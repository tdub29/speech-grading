
# William Zinsser: Clutter-Cutting Revision

## When to use

Use when the user asks to tighten, simplify, de-jargon, make prose less wordy, remove fluff, or make a paragraph sound human without changing its meaning.

Don't trigger for broad prose improvement when another skill owns the artifact. This skill should end in the concrete output named below.

## The framework

- Zinsser's operating rule is that clear writing is the result of subtraction: examine every word for whether it performs necessary work.
- Clutter includes obvious redundancies, inflated nouns, prepositional padding, hedges, qualifiers, fad words, and institutional euphemism that hides responsibility.
- The test is not minimalism for its own sake. Cut until the sentence says exactly what it means in words a real person would use.

## How to apply

1. Bracket every removable phrase: throat-clearing, 'it is important to note,' repeated context, defensive qualifiers, and filler transitions.
2. Replace pompous diction with plain nouns and verbs: 'utilize' to 'use,' 'facilitate' to 'help,' 'at this point in time' to 'now.'
3. Collapse noun stacks into verbs: 'make an assessment of' becomes 'assess'; 'provide assistance to' becomes 'help.'
4. Remove hedges unless they carry real uncertainty. Keep 'may' when probability matters; cut it when it is only politeness or fear.
5. Read the tightened sentence aloud. If the rhythm sounds clipped or meaning changed, restore the smallest word group that fixes it.
6. Return a before/after plus a short rationale for the top cuts so the writer can repeat the pattern.

## Common failure modes

- Cutting facts instead of clutter. Zinsser cuts fog, not evidence.
- Replacing human plainness with telegraphic austerity. A sentence can be short and still dead.
- Leaving the nominalization while deleting only adjectives around it.
- Treating all modifiers as clutter. Specific modifiers that sharpen image or evidence should stay.
- Letting legal/corporate caution survive as anonymous passive evasions.
- Changing the writer's meaning to win a word-count contest.

## Output artifact

Produce: (1) clutter inventory by type, (2) marked-up deletion list, (3) tightened rewrite, (4) one transferable rule for the writer's next draft.

Use this compact structure:

```markdown
## Clutter-Cutting Revision

### Diagnosis
[What is failing and why]

### Revision Plan
[Concrete edits/checks in priority order]

### Rewrite
[Before/after or revised passage]

### Watchouts
[Failure modes most relevant here]
```
