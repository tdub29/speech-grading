
# Roy Peter Clark: Verb Engine Rewrite

## When to use

Use when the user asks to strengthen verbs, make sentences move, improve energy, or fix slow openings.

Don't trigger for broad prose improvement when another skill owns the artifact. This skill should end in the concrete output named below.

## The framework

- Clark's first tools treat the sentence as a machine: subject and verb provide the engine, and strong verbs create motion.
- Delayed subjects and weak verbs make readers carry too much memory load before they know what is happening.
- The practical edit is often mechanical: find the actor, find the action, move them forward, then preserve the best emphasis at the end.

## How to apply

1. Underline every main verb. Circle be/do/have/make constructions and nominalized actions.
2. Mark the distance from sentence start to subject and main verb. Anything long gets a delay warning.
3. Replace weak verb phrases with precise verbs where meaning allows.
4. Convert passive or expletive openings only when the actor matters and the passive is not doing useful focus work.
5. Check the sentence ending: after moving the engine forward, keep the strongest word or phrase in the stress position.
6. Return a table: original engine, problem, revised engine, effect.

## Common failure modes

- Blindly banning passive voice. Clark wants force, not grammar superstition.
- Choosing flashy verbs that distort the actual action.
- Moving subject and verb forward but stranding the important word in the middle.
- Treating every 'to be' verb as weak; sometimes identity or state is the point.
- Fixing one sentence at a time and creating monotonous rhythm across the paragraph.
- Replacing clear nouns with vague verbs just to make a sentence feel active.

## Output artifact

Produce: verb-engine audit table, revised passage, and three strongest verb substitutions with rationale.

Use this compact structure:

```markdown
## Verb Engine Rewrite

### Diagnosis
[What is failing and why]

### Revision Plan
[Concrete edits/checks in priority order]

### Rewrite
[Before/after or revised passage]

### Watchouts
[Failure modes most relevant here]
```
