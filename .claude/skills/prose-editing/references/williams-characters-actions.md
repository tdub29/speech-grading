
# Williams & Bizup: Characters and Actions Revision

## When to use

Use when a paragraph is grammatically correct but hard to understand because agency and action are buried.

Don't trigger for broad prose improvement when another skill owns the artifact. This skill should end in the concrete output named below.

## The framework

- Readers understand stories through characters and actions, even in technical, legal, academic, or business prose.
- Nominalizations often bury actions in nouns; abstract subjects often hide the characters readers need.
- Passive voice is not automatically wrong. It is wrong when it hides a character the reader needs or makes the action harder to track.

## How to apply

1. Underline nominalizations and abstract nouns that encode actions: implementation, analysis, decision, failure, increase.
2. Ask who or what performs each buried action. Reconstruct missing characters where the draft omits them.
3. Make the main character the grammatical subject unless the old information, topic flow, or ethics require another focus.
4. Make the important action the verb, not a noun supported by 'make/do/provide/conduct.'
5. Evaluate passive clauses: keep when the actor is unknown, irrelevant, obvious, or when the patient is the topic; revise when it evades responsibility.
6. Return a before/after and explain which subjects/actions now carry the sentence.

## Common failure modes

- Mechanical active-voice conversion that damages topic flow.
- Inventing actors not supported by evidence.
- Keeping abstract subjects like 'the implementation of the policy' when readers need people or systems.
- Turning every noun into a verb and losing useful concepts.
- Using passive to hide responsibility in ethical or accountability contexts.
- Fixing sentence one but leaving later sentences with incompatible characters.

## Output artifact

Produce: nominalization list, character/action reconstruction table, passive keep/revise decisions, and a clarified rewrite.

Use this compact structure:

