
# William Zinsser: Human Audience Pass

## When to use

Use when the user wants a memo, article, explainer, update, or essay to sound more alive, personal, direct, or reader-aware without becoming casual slop.

Don't trigger for broad prose improvement when another skill owns the artifact. This skill should end in the concrete output named below.

## The framework

- Zinsser argues that writers do not write to a demographic blob; they write as themselves to one reader they respect.
- Style is the writer's humanity under discipline: warmth and confidence without performing personality.
- Business and expert writing fail when they hide the actor, the reader, or the real stakes behind institutional nouns.

## How to apply

1. Name the real reader: one role, one situation, one reason they are reading now.
2. Name the writer's legitimate presence: witness, expert, colleague, guide, advocate, or narrator.
3. Replace institutional abstractions with people doing things where accountability matters.
4. Keep one or two human details that earn trust; cut personality flourishes that distract from the reader's need.
5. Check tone against respect: not patronizing, not performatively folksy, not hiding behind jargon.
6. Rewrite one representative passage in a one-human-to-one-human stance.

## Common failure modes

- Writing for 'the audience' so broadly that no sentence knows who it serves.
- Removing all first person and accidentally removing accountability.
- Adding jokes or chatty fillers instead of genuine human presence.
- Mistaking corporate caution for professionalism.
- Overexplaining basics because the writer does not trust the reader.
- Voice-polishing before the piece has a point. Run `references/zinsser-unity-contract.md` first if the contract is unclear.

## Output artifact

Produce: audience profile, writer stance, facelessness flags, accountability rewrites, and one polished humanized passage.

Use this compact structure:

```markdown
## Human Audience Pass

### Diagnosis
[What is failing and why]

### Revision Plan
[Concrete edits/checks in priority order]

### Rewrite
[Before/after or revised passage]

### Watchouts
[Failure modes most relevant here]
```
