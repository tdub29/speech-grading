
# Williams & Bizup: Cohesion and Coherence Map

## When to use

Use when users say 'the sentences are fine but it doesn't flow,' 'this jumps around,' or 'the argument is hard to follow.'

Don't trigger for broad prose improvement when another skill owns the artifact. This skill should end in the concrete output named below.

## The framework

- Cohesion is the reader's sense that each sentence connects to the one before it, usually by beginning with familiar information.
- Coherence is the reader's sense that a sequence of sentences is about a stable set of topics moving toward a point.
- Readers experience coherence; writers create it by managing openings, topic strings, transitions, and paragraph purpose.

## How to apply

1. Mark each sentence opening as old/familiar or new/unintroduced.
2. Track topic subjects across sentences. Identify sudden topic shifts and fake transitions that only paste over jumps.
3. Move familiar/contextual material toward sentence beginnings and new/complex material toward endings.
4. Group sentences by shared topic; split paragraphs when two topic chains compete.
5. Add explicit transitions only where the logical relation remains unclear after reordering.
6. Write a one-sentence point for each paragraph and verify every sentence contributes.

## Common failure modes

- Adding however/therefore while the underlying topic chain remains broken.
- Beginning every sentence with a new abstraction and forcing readers to rebuild context.
- Overusing repeated keywords without logical development; repetition is not coherence by itself.
- Pursuing local flow while the paragraph lacks a point.
- Starting with old information that is old to the writer but not to the reader.
- Splitting paragraphs by length rather than by topic movement.

## Output artifact

Produce: old/new sentence map, topic-chain diagnosis, paragraph point statements, and a reordered/coherent rewrite.

Use this compact structure:

```markdown
## Cohesion and Coherence Map

### Diagnosis
[What is failing and why]

### Revision Plan
[Concrete edits/checks in priority order]

### Rewrite
[Before/after or revised passage]

### Watchouts
[Failure modes most relevant here]
```
