
# William Zinsser: Unity Contract

## When to use

Use when an article, memo, essay, post, or report feels unfocused, overstuffed, or inconsistent in point of view.

Don't trigger for broad prose improvement when another skill owns the artifact. This skill should end in the concrete output named below.

## The framework

- Zinsser treats unity as a set of writer's decisions made before and during drafting: what one point, what pronoun, what tense, what mood, what scope.
- A unified piece can contain variety, but every paragraph must serve the same reader promise.
- The most useful revision is often not improving stray material but removing material that belongs to a different article.

## How to apply

1. State the one-sentence reader contract: 'This piece shows [audience] how/why [specific thing].'
2. Check pronoun and stance: I/we/you/they. Flag shifts that change the relationship with the reader.
3. Check tense and time frame. Mark unexplained jumps from present analysis to past narrative to future speculation.
4. Check scope creep. For each section, label it core, support, tangent, or different-piece material.
5. Check mood and level of formality. Identify paragraphs that suddenly become academic, salesy, jokey, or defensive.
6. Produce a keep/cut/reframe map and a revised outline that honors the contract.

## Common failure modes

- Solving focus problems with better transitions instead of cutting the off-contract material.
- Letting research notes dictate structure after the reader contract has changed.
- Confusing unity with monotony; examples can vary if they point to the same promise.
- Changing pronouns for variety and accidentally changing accountability.
- Keeping a beloved anecdote because it is good in isolation, though it belongs in another piece.
- Writing the thesis after the draft but not revising the body to match it.

## Output artifact

Produce: unity contract, stance/tense/mood diagnostics, section-by-section keep/cut/reframe table, and a revised outline.

Use this compact structure:

```markdown
## Unity Contract

### Diagnosis
[What is failing and why]

### Revision Plan
[Concrete edits/checks in priority order]

### Rewrite
[Before/after or revised passage]

### Watchouts
[Failure modes most relevant here]
```
