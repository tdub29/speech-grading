
# Roy Peter Clark: Nut Graf and Gold Coins Blueprint

## When to use

Use when a draft has anecdotes and facts but lacks a clear 'so what,' section momentum, or memorable ending.

Don't trigger for broad prose improvement when another skill owns the artifact. This skill should end in the concrete output named below.

## The framework

- Clark's nut graf tells readers what the story is about and why they should keep going, especially after an anecdotal lead.
- Gold coins are rewards placed along the trail: vivid facts, scenes, quotes, turns, or insights that repay attention.
- Endings are not just stops. The final coin should feel earned by the path already laid.

## How to apply

1. Write the current nut graf in one sentence. If none exists, infer what readers would think the story promises.
2. Check whether the nut graf names topic, tension, stakes, and scope without killing curiosity.
3. Map sections as a reader path. Mark every gold coin already present: surprising fact, scene, quote, image, insight, reversal.
4. Find reward droughts: long stretches of explanation with no coin.
5. Move or add coins so each section gives readers a reason to continue.
6. Design the ending as the final coin: echo, reveal, image, implication, or changed understanding.

## Common failure modes

- Writing a nut graf that summarizes the whole piece so completely readers can stop.
- Burying the nut graf after readers have already become confused.
- Using gold coins as trivia unrelated to the main path.
- Front-loading all the best material and leaving the middle unrewarded.
- Adding a twist ending unsupported by the story's evidence.
- Confusing clickbait suspense with genuine reader orientation.

## Output artifact

Produce: current/inferred nut graf, revised nut graf, section reward map, drought fixes, and ending recommendation.

Use this compact structure:

```markdown
## Nut Graf and Gold Coins Blueprint

### Diagnosis
[What is failing and why]

### Revision Plan
[Concrete edits/checks in priority order]

### Rewrite
[Before/after or revised passage]

### Watchouts
[Failure modes most relevant here]
```
