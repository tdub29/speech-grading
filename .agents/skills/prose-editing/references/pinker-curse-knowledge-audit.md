
# Steven Pinker: Curse of Knowledge Audit

## When to use

Use when explaining expert material to a mixed, new, or external audience.

Don't trigger for broad prose improvement when another skill owns the artifact. This skill should end in the concrete output named below.

## The framework

- The curse of knowledge is the difficulty of imagining what it is like not to know what you know.
- Writers omit steps not because they are careless, but because knowledge makes those steps invisible.
- The cure is not dumbing down. It is reconstructing the reader's path from what they know to what the writer wants them to see.

## How to apply

1. Define the target reader's starting knowledge: terms they know, terms they may have heard, terms they cannot know yet.
2. Highlight unexplained acronyms, insider labels, compressed causal jumps, and references to unstated context.
3. Mark false-obvious words: 'clearly,' 'obviously,' 'of course,' 'simply,' and 'just.'
4. Insert missing bridges: definition, example, contrast, reason, or consequence.
5. Replace expert category labels with an explanation first, label second, when introducing a concept.
6. Test the rewrite by reading only the repaired bridge sentences; they should form a path.

## Common failure modes

- Adding a glossary while leaving the inferential chain broken.
- Overexplaining to experts because the target reader was never defined.
- Using examples that require the same background knowledge as the concept.
- Treating confusion as a vocabulary problem when the missing link is causal or structural.
- Leaving 'obviously' in place as a signal that a hidden step exists.
- Flattening technical precision instead of sequencing it.

## Output artifact

Produce: target-reader baseline, knowledge-gap list, bridge-type table, and a repaired explanation passage.

Use this compact structure:

```markdown
## Curse of Knowledge Audit

### Diagnosis
[What is failing and why]

### Revision Plan
[Concrete edits/checks in priority order]

### Rewrite
[Before/after or revised passage]

### Watchouts
[Failure modes most relevant here]
```
