
# Steven Pinker: Coherence Arcs

## When to use

Use when the user asks why an argument or explanation does not hang together even though the sentences are clear.

Don't trigger for broad prose improvement when another skill owns the artifact. This skill should end in the concrete output named below.

## The framework

- Readers hunger for coherence and infer relations even when writers do not state them.
- A coherent passage has arcs connecting entities, events, claims, causes, contrasts, examples, and implications across sentences.
- Connectives help only when they name the real relation; a wrong connective is worse than none.

## How to apply

1. List each sentence or section as a proposition.
2. Label the relation to the previous unit: cause, effect, contrast, concession, example, elaboration, sequence, generalization, problem-solution, or topic return.
3. Mark missing or false relations where readers must guess or where the transition lies.
4. Track repeated entities and themes across the passage; identify dropped or suddenly introduced topics.
5. Add, remove, or replace connectives to make real relations explicit.
6. Reorder units where relation labels reveal a better path.

## Common failure modes

- Using 'therefore' for mere sequence or 'however' for a topic change.
- Letting readers infer a causal relation the evidence does not support.
- Adding transition words to a passage that lacks an argument path.
- Dropping a key entity and reintroducing it with a synonym readers may not connect.
- Creating proportion problems: spending five paragraphs on a minor branch and one sentence on the main claim.
- Treating coherence as local sentence flow only; arcs operate across whole sections.

## Output artifact

Produce: proposition list, coherence-relation map, false/missing connective flags, reordered outline, and revised connective plan.

Use this compact structure:

```markdown
## Coherence Arcs

### Diagnosis
[What is failing and why]

### Revision Plan
[Concrete edits/checks in priority order]

### Rewrite
[Before/after or revised passage]

### Watchouts
[Failure modes most relevant here]
```
