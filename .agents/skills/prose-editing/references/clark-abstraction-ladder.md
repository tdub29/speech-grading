
# Roy Peter Clark: Ladder of Abstraction

## When to use

Use when the user says a passage feels vague, too theoretical, too anecdotal, or lacks a bridge from detail to significance.

Don't trigger for broad prose improvement when another skill owns the artifact. This skill should end in the concrete output named below.

## The framework

- Clark's ladder runs from low rungs (names, scenes, sensory details, examples) to high rungs (ideas, categories, values, themes).
- Good nonfiction climbs and descends. Staying high becomes fog; staying low becomes anecdote without meaning.
- The edit is to locate missing rungs: the concrete proof below an abstraction or the explanatory idea above a detail.

## How to apply

1. Extract the highest-level claim in the passage. Write it as a plain sentence.
2. List the lowest-rung details: names, numbers, objects, quotes, scenes, actions.
3. Mark gaps where a claim has no concrete rung or a detail has no interpretive rung.
4. Add one concrete example below each unsupported abstraction.
5. Add one interpretive sentence above each orphan anecdote that explains why it matters.
6. Sequence the paragraph to climb or descend intentionally, not randomly.

## Common failure modes

- Adding decorative examples that do not prove the abstract claim.
- Explaining every detail so heavily that readers cannot infer anything.
- Using jargon as a fake high rung; real abstraction clarifies category or significance.
- Staying on the ladder's middle rungs with bland labels and no vivid proof.
- Treating one anecdote as universal proof without bounds.
- Climbing to a moral that the evidence does not support.

## Output artifact

Produce: ladder table, missing-rung diagnosis, added concrete/proposition sentences, and a revised paragraph that climbs or descends cleanly.

Use this compact structure:

```markdown
## Ladder of Abstraction

### Diagnosis
[What is failing and why]

### Revision Plan
[Concrete edits/checks in priority order]

### Rewrite
[Before/after or revised passage]

### Watchouts
[Failure modes most relevant here]
```
