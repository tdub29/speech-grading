
# William Zinsser: Lead and Ending Audit

## When to use

Use when the user says the opening is boring, the ending is flat, the piece starts too slowly, or they don't know how to close.

Don't trigger for broad prose improvement when another skill owns the artifact. This skill should end in the concrete output named below.

## The framework

- Zinsser's lead must invite the reader into the exact journey the piece will take, not merely announce a topic.
- The ending should stop where the piece's energy naturally resolves. It should feel inevitable, not bolted on.
- A good lead and ending often echo each other indirectly through image, question, scene, or idea rather than through summary.

## How to apply

1. Identify the real first live sentence: the first place the draft becomes specific, surprising, human, or consequential.
2. Cut throat-clearing before that sentence unless a tiny setup is required for intelligibility.
3. Classify the lead type: scene, question, startling fact, compressed anecdote, promise, or problem. Check whether it matches the piece's contract.
4. Audit the ending for four traps: summary, sermon, fade-out, and new-topic swerve.
5. Find a closing return: image, phrase, unanswered tension resolved, or final concrete detail that carries the meaning.
6. Offer two lead options and two ending options, each with the tradeoff it makes.

## Common failure modes

- Starting with the assignment instead of the reader's reason to care.
- Using a clever lead that promises a different piece than the body delivers.
- Saving the strongest concrete detail for paragraph four and making readers earn their way to it.
- Ending with 'In conclusion' energy: restating instead of landing.
- Adding a grand lesson the piece has not earned.
- Closing with a new idea that should have been developed earlier.

## Output artifact

Produce: buried-lead finding, lead diagnosis, two rewritten leads, ending trap diagnosis, two rewritten endings, and a note on which pair best fits the unity contract.

Use this compact structure:

```markdown

[What is failing and why]

```
